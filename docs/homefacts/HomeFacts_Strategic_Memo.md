## Executive summary

HomeFacts is best understood not as a real-estate or property-information site, but as an established **place-intelligence platform** that naturally complements IDLookup's **identity-intelligence platform**.

The connecting insight is that **the address is the shared graph node**:

> HomeFacts explains **places**. IDLookup explains **people**. Joined on the address, they explain the relationships between people, properties, neighborhoods, ownership, public records, risk, and historical events.

Two things make this actionable rather than aspirational:

1. **We've already removed the largest execution risk.** IDLookup has recreated the HomeFacts experience as a working MVP — [idlookup.me/homefacts](https://idlookup.me/homefacts) — that matches HomeFacts module-for-module, adds a dozen it doesn't have, and delivers the one thing property data can't: *who lives here.* The conversation is no longer "can this be built?" but "can we create more value together?"
2. **We understand how HomeFacts actually makes money** (below), so a partnership can be structured to be strictly *additive* to its existing revenue — provably, not rhetorically.

**Recommended path:** a focused, de-risked commercial **pilot** first; broader partnership if it proves out; acquisition as a *future* option once the economics are validated — never the opening ask.

## What HomeFacts actually is

Its durable assets are an **address-keyed information graph**, not listings:

- Address hierarchy (state → county → city → ZIP → address) with a mature SEO footprint
- Neighborhood intelligence, demographics, schools
- Crime, sex-offender, and public-safety data
- Environmental and natural-hazard data
- Historical property and neighborhood context

This is the same graph IDLookup builds from the identity side. The overlap is structural, not superficial.

### How it makes money today (verified from HomeFacts' own pages)

- **Display advertising — the primary model.** HomeFacts' own advertising page quotes **~2.5M unique visitors/mo, 7M+ page views, 28M+ ad impressions/mo**, sold to home-buying-cycle advertisers (real-estate, mortgage, legal, financial). Direct-sold banner/text, national + geo-targeted local.
- **Programmatic ads** via a third-party ad wrapper (Cordless Media) layered on the record pages.
- **A person-search / "background check" feature** on the offenders and record pages — the person-intent stream, currently monetized thinly relative to what that intent is worth.
- Mobile apps (iOS + Android).

**Why this matters:** the person-intent traffic and the ad inventory monetize *different intents on the same page*. IDLookup's funnel monetizes the former; HomeFacts' ad business keeps the latter untouched. And because our work rebuilds the SEO, it **grows the total traffic the display ads are sold against** — the ad business gets bigger, not smaller. This is the difference between "cannibalizing your revenue" and "adding a second revenue line while feeding your first."

## The opportunity

- **Mature but declining SEO = the opportunity, not a contradiction.** The address footprint carries real, hard-won authority; its organic traffic is quietly eroding because no one can act on the site. Arresting and reversing that decline is precisely what our team does — and it lifts both the person-search funnel *and* the ad impressions.
- **Person-intent is under-monetized.** High-value person-name and neighborhood-safety searches convert far better through a purpose-built people-search funnel than through display ads or a thin background-check feature.
- **The "who lives here" moat.** Reverse-address → residents (privacy-masked, opt-out honored) is the join property data cannot answer on its own. It's the clearest proof that identity + place is worth more than either alone.

## Deal shape & sequencing

**Pilot first (concrete, de-risked):** redirect a defined slice of person-intent traffic (e.g., sex-offender and person-name pages) into the IDLookup funnel for 30–60 days, measured apples-to-apples against the current baseline. Zero cost, zero engineering, fully reversible, no change to the ad business. (See the one-page pilot proposal.)

**Then expand:** run and rebuild HomeFacts' SEO end-to-end; broader identity × place integration; revenue share on lifetime value.

**Acquisition — future option only,** after validating: customer conversion, traffic quality/durability, licensing economics (ATTOM), technical integration, and legal/compliance. A successful partnership substantially de-risks any later acquisition.

### How to sequence this with David Teng — important

Teng currently reads HomeFacts as a **cost with minimal upside that he can't even modify.** That mindset dictates the order of the conversation:

- **Lead with the concrete, not the vision.** Open on "a cost line becomes a revenue line, at zero cost or effort to NIC," backed by the working MVP and the measurable pilot. The place-intelligence/address-graph vision is the **payoff you unveil once he's leaning in — not the opener.** Leading with "unified consumer intelligence platform" to a cost-minded operator raises his guard; leading with "here's how this makes money at no risk" lowers it.
- **The MVP is the single strongest opening point** — it converts the discussion from "can we build this?" to "can we make more together?"
- **Don't open by negotiating terms** either. The first ask is a 20–30 minute conversation and a small pilot, not a rev-share percentage.

The vision in this memo is the right *destination*; the pilot is the right *on-ramp*.

## Risks

| Risk | Read |
|---|---|
| **ATTOM licensing** | The one real data gap (parcel/property detail). A partnership hook, not a blocker — resolvable via ATTOM or NIC's existing rights. |
| **Technical carve-out complexity** | Largely neutralized — the MVP proves we can run the experience on our own infrastructure without touching NIC's codebase. |
| **Organic-traffic durability** | Real. Mitigated by active SEO ownership; the pilot measures traffic quality before any commitment. |
| **Privacy / compliance** | Person data is masked and opt-out-honored on public pages; standard people-search compliance obligations apply and are already part of IDLookup's operating model. |

---

## Recommendation

Pursue a **strategic partnership, pilot-first.** Use the MVP as proof that engineering risk is off the table, and structure the pilot to be provably additive to HomeFacts' ad revenue. Lead Teng with the concrete; hold the place × identity vision as the destination the pilot earns its way toward. Keep acquisition as a future option, unlocked by commercial validation rather than argued for up front.
